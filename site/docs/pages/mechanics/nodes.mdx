# Resource nodes

*Between the two keeps lie the old workings — a forge still warm, a quarry still open, a grove still standing. Whoever holds them eats.*

Three resource nodes sit on the map between the two players:

- **Forge** (`nc0`) — iron and linen
- **Quarry** (`nc1`) — stone and wood
- **Grove** (`nc2`) — ember and seeds

At any moment, each node has a single controller, or no one. Control isn't taken by violence — it's won quietly, through contests that resolve alongside the gate math.

## Contesting

Each round you can allocate budget to the `nc0`, `nc1`, and `nc2` slots to contest a node. When the round resolves, whoever spent more on a node wins control for the **next** round. Ties favor the status quo — the current controller keeps the node if spending matches. A contest is a commitment: the points you spend here are points you don't spend on gates or defense.

## Rewards for control

Controlling a node pays out at the end of each round:

- **Budget bonus.** Each controlled node adds +1 to your budget next round. Three nodes is three extra points — meaningful, compounding, and hard to claw back once it tips.
- **Resource tokens.** Each controlled node mints 1 of each of its paired tokens to you at round-end. These are real ERC-20 tokens, they persist across matches, and you'll use them to craft abilities. See [Resources](/mechanics/resources).

## Nodes are orthogonal to gates

Contesting a node doesn't damage your opponent. It doesn't interact with the gate math at all. Nodes are a parallel economic layer — a long game running under the short game. A player who loses every gate round but holds two nodes will often outlast an opponent who wins gates but leaves the map empty.

## Traps live on nodes

Nodes you own can be trapped — at a cost, and with a real trade-off. See [Traps](/mechanics/traps) for how trapping interacts with contesting.
